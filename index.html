Rivet Health / Zelis

Contract Negotiation Suite Redesign

Goal: reduce churn for payer performance products by rebuilding the contract negotiation workflow end to end.

Role
Lead Product Designer / Researcher
Company
Rivet Health (acquired by Zelis)
Timeline
2024 – ongoing

Problem

The Payer Performance's churn rate was volatile throughout 2024, averaging 34% and peaking at 45% in October. In order to turn this around, we wanted to focus was on the contract negotiation tools, which had not had any updates since their launch in 2019.

Hypotheses for why churn rate was so high:

  • Many of the products had usability issues
  • Several of the products had bugs
  • Not clear how to use all the services together
  • Some pages or services seem didn't need to be a separate tool

Customer churn rates

34%
Avg churn for 2024
31%
Churn at Modeler launch - Apr 2025
21%
Churn at Benchmark v2 launch - Feb 2026
18%
Churn in Jun 2026

User for payer contract management

  • Revenue cycle managers
  • Account execs
  • Director of Business Operations
  • Consultants
  • C-suite, Admins, Management, Contracting Team

User interviews & usability studies

As lead project researcher, we wanted to decrease churn. After reviewing all the separate services, I recommended we understand the whole contract negotiation process, end-to-end. This would help inform how to shape our redesigns and where we should draw the line between services. The pages included:

Pages or services for contract negotiation

  • Modeler
  • Benchmark tool
  • SuperScore and Individual Payer Scorecard page
  • Pricing Optimization tool
  • Renewals and Expirations page
  • Contract Terms
  • Procedure Comparison (Bubbles)
  • All rates

Interview & usability findings

Below are the findings of all the interviews for the contract negotiation suite of products. The PM and I categorized them into themes and then into the different general phases of the contract negotiation process.

User Journey map

A journey map is a visualization of the process that a person goes through in order to accomplish a goal.

This journey map included more concrete phases, pain points the user might encounter, and any other tools the user may use outside of Rivet.

  • Awareness - do I need to start negotiations with a payer and when
  • Preparation - how do my rates look compared to CMS baseline and my competitors
  • Action - I stated negotiations and will evaluate what those outcomes might mean for my business. Uses will go back and forth at least 8 times.
  • Result - Once I have agree on rates with a payer, papers will be signed and put into place.

Findings from research

After looking at the journey map, it was much clearer how to organize what the user goals for that step is, and how the jobs to be done would fit into these services. The biggest finding being that we could fold in 3 of the separate services into existing services. which helps to decrease confusion for customers as they don't need to navigate through so many different services.

Detailed findings:

  • Many of the products had usability issues and bugs
  • Confusing for users to understand how to use services together. Several customers never using many of the services but remember being shown them during onboarding.
  • After understanding personas, user goals, and steps to achieve those goals, it was clear some stand alone services could be folded into some existing services, like pricing optimization tool, renewals and expirations, and procedure comparison
  • We found areas to help connect the dots for customers:
    • Updating contract expiration date when implementing a model
    • Get notified prior to contract expiration
    • Way to implement a contract with escalators (3 year contract that changes a bit each years)
    • Way to put a date in to start new rates and a way to know if rates and correctly pulling from the updated fee schedule
    • Way to find contracts for customers. Many customer are not able to locate their contracts - which was a surpsurprising pain point.
    • Leveraging AI to negotiate, like drafting letter to payer
    • Way to analyze contract language for problematic terms like evergreen clause

Service restructuring

Pricing optimization tool → All rates

Procedure comparison → All rates

Renewals & expirations → Superscore

Outcomes

This project is ongoing with redesign of Modeler V2 launching in April 2025 and Benchmark V2 following in February 2026. There is another redesign in the works as well.

Business impact:

  • Churn dropped from a 2024 average of 34% to 18% by June 2026 — a 16 percentage point reduction tied directly to launch milestones
  • Modeler MAUs nearly doubled in the 12 months following launch (+92% YoY)
  • Benchmark V2 saw an all-time product high of 127 MAUs, reached within weeks of launch in March 2026.

Rivet Health / Zelis

Modeler Redesign

Goal: redesign Modeler, increase usage of the tool, and decrease customer churn for Rivet users.

Role
Product Designer
Company
Rivet Health (acquired by Zelis)
Launched
April 1, 2025

Overview

Modeler allows users to compare current and proposed changes to fee schedules, multiplied by volume. Revenue can be viewed by individual procedure codes, procedure code groups, rendering providers, or provider taxonomy. This tool helps practices prepare for payer negotiations by modeling the revenue impact of proposed rate changes.

Modeler needed a redesign — it was buggy, slow, and in some places broken. Users had also requested additional features like applying modifiers and adjustments to calculations, and the ability to use claims data for real-time volume numbers.

Business impact

+92%
MAU in the 12 months following launch
−11 pts
Decrease in customer churn from Apr 2025 to Jan 2026

Findings from previous research

  • Not clear to users when to clone, select, or upload
  • Users found that the fee schedule builder was too cramped, scrolling in a small space when trying to edit rates
  • User tried and failed to use dragging and dropping a rule
  • Users have to hover over each category or code to see the details + impact
  • Users wanted to see all this info together
  • In order to see all codes or categories today, a user has to side scroll for a long time. And when they get to the end, it doesn't even show all the codes
  • Users were confused about how to select utilization
  • Confusion around why the tool requires utilization files when Audit customers have claims in Rivet, they want to be able to just use claims data
  • Unclear if adjustments are being applied to the calculations
  • Want to compare more than one FS at time
  • Want to apply MPPR (adjustment rules)
  • Want to be able to edit utilization
  • Have additional filtering options like place of service, etc
  • Want to compare baselines, including older baselines
  • Want to see blended baseline comparison
  • Fix general bugs like clone timing out and upload large files timing out
  • Bugs
  • Page loads very slow
  • Often surfaces an error around utilization but reloading the page fixes it
  • Sometimes the clone feature looks like it does not work, but then you close the modal and it worked
  • Large utilization file upload times out
  • Wanted to see by specialty or procedure code
  • Wanted to see older CMS baselines

Process

The redesign followed six phases: interviews and findings, wireframes, stakeholder review, usability sessions, incorporating feedback, and final fidelity designs.

User flows for tiers

Two user flows for the different user tiers: Maximize and Audit. The main difference is that Maximize users do not have claims data available to use for volume.

Usability findings on wireframes

Biggest question: how do we define this product, we know a user wants to see rates & utilization but is there a use case where they want to see rates without it?

Templates

  • People were confused by templates, not enough distinct use cases, several of the categories were not useful with no utilization. Helped us to get clarity around what this tool is used to measure without utilization, you will be able to do this in the all rates page.

CMS Baselines

  • Loved that we included CMS baselines to compare
  • Users want percentage of Medicare calculations as a standard feature
  • Clear need to compare proposals against Medicare benchmarks to understand relative value

Utilization

  • All participants emphasized that utilization data is crucial for meaningful analysis
    • One user stated that fee schedule comparisons without utilization data are not valuable - "I don't know why I would use fee schedule comparisons without utilization"
    • One user wanted to manipulate the utilization data

Adjustments

  • Users want all adjustments applied but prefer flexibility to toggle them on/off
  • Valerie valued the speed of utilization file processing compared to previous experiences
  • Most users prefer faster approximations over waiting for perfect accuracy in MPPR calculations
  • Want visibility into how adjustments affect calculations

UI

  • Positive response to the comparison view "much cleaner than the first model"
  • Users requested improved filtering by provider name, facility, and code categories
  • Several participants wanted code ranges alongside category names for better context
  • Interest in exportable summary data and visualizations for presentation to leadership (way to download data)
  • Include code ranges alongside category names for better context

General

  • Users typically create multiple drafts (some mentioned needing 10-30 drafts for complex negotiations)
  • Request for ability to clone drafts across different models
  • Current pain point: "Biggest gripe I have with modeler is comparing these two schedules is hard"
  • Users want clear baseline identification throughout the interface
"Enhancements made to the modeler tool have significantly improved its usability and efficiency."
- Customer feedback
"One of the most delightful experiences we have in Rivet is the new modeler. It seems that every time I use it with a customer I get asked some seemingly edge case and somehow, magically, not only does that feature exist but it just works."
- Andrew Harding, Rivet Co-founder

Outcomes

Modeler V2 launched April 1, 2025 — a full redesign of one of Rivet's most used contract negotiation tools.

The results so far:

  • MAUs nearly doubled in the 12 months following launch, with a +92% year-over-year increase
  • Customer churn dropped 11 points - from 31% at launch to 20% by January 2026
  • March 2026 saw an all-time product high of 127 MAUs across the Payer Performance suite

Beyond the numbers, the redesign resolved foundational usability issues that had existed since the original launch — clarifying how utilization works, surfacing visibility, and making fee schedule comparison dramatically easier.

Benchmark Tool Enhancement

Goal: add more functionality to Benchmark, including an improved paid data source for Rivet users.

Role
Product Designer
Company
Rivet Health (acquired by Zelis)
Launched
Feb 2026 (V2), Jun 2026 (follow-ups)

Overview

After the 'Lower Costs, More Transparency Act' was put into law, healthcare practices were required to publish their rates publicly. Rivet ingested this data and built Benchmark — an exploratory tool that gives practices powerful insights into market pricing. It helps answer questions like:

  • How do my rates compare to others in my area?
  • How do my rates compare to specific practices I compete with?

Benchmark is a key part of contract negotiation. When a practice finds that competitors are being paid more for specific codes, they can use that as leverage to negotiate better contracts.

For V2, we had two goals: add functionality users had been requesting since launch, and upgrade to a cleaner data source from a company called Serif. The improved data quality was foundational — better data meant more reliable comparisons and more confident negotiations for our users.

Business impact

−3 pts
Decrease in customer churn 4 months after launch (June 2026)
127
March 2026 saw highest MAU

Improvements based on original findings

  • Way to search for 5 codes, practices, providers at a time
  • Way to pivot that data
  • See CMS locality relative to published rates
  • Browse codes by category — i.e., quickly find an anesthesia code
  • Filter by provider credential
  • Save views - filter sets
  • See rates associated with specific service codes
  • See if modifiers are applied or not
  • Be able to select multiple Tax IDs, multiple NPIs
  • Automatically select NPIs and Tax IDs when user select provider or practice
  • We had an opportunity to purchase cleaner data from a company called Serif, which would help with data cleanliness
  • Want to see where there are a concentration of rates
  • Easier way to get data to show up
  • Way to favorite a provider or practice
  • Clear indicator of when you are selecting your data and comparison data
  • Way to save a view (filter set)

Finding the right problems

Heard from customers (and from co-founder) that customers wanted a way to favorite a data. A way to be able to pin/star/favorite certain practices.

I wanted to understand the why behind this. At this time, it was very complex to find the right data files to show up in benchmark, or any data sometimes. This is because a practice can have multiple Tax IDs or type 2 NPIs associated with it. It can be hard to capture all the needed files to get a complete view. One solution was to use a rule builder pattern to add all the needed data sources for a practice and save it. I pushed back on this solution because there is no way for a user to know when all the correct files are selected.

The PM and I spoke with our eng team spoke with out eng team and turns out, there was a way for us to find all related type 1 and type 2 NPIs associated with a payer or practice and automatically select them for the payer. This as a game changer! No need for crazy complexity, and it turned out, we didn't need the favoriting feature any longer.

Usability findings

Main takeaways

  • Users did not understand whisker plot
  • Want to understand if their rates are included in the "market average" and other metrics
  • Way to see all rates at one time, not mouse over each one at once
  • Understand why I am seeing multiple rates at once
  • For histogram, show range in popover
  • Be able to select multiple Tax IDs, multiple NPIs
  • See CMS locality relative to published rates at once
  • Include CMS baseline rates by locality

Onboarding

  • Make the distinction clearer between your data vs competitor data
  • If user picks their own tax ID, way to narrow down by location, address, state
  • Search by name of business (DBA) name, found this data was available from eng
  • Want to see where there are a concentration of rates
  • Want a way to compare CMS baseline to a FS
  • Want to be able to find business that are similar in size to them

Filters

  • Add in extra filters types
    • Plan type: HMO, PPO
    • Facility vs non-facility (service code type)
  • Add credentials to provider dropdown menu
  • Show facet counts in dropdown menu when selecting Tax ID, NPI, to know there is child data associated
  • Show NPIs associated with the Tax ID that is YOUR data
  • Allow users to review type 2 NPI, keep relevant NPIs and deselect ones that aren't relevant
  • Show all filters at top, instead of adding filters
  • Make clearer when using type 1 or type 2 NPI
  • Add way to search by locality for CBSA or GPCI (geographic region)
  • Want to be able to find business that are similar in size to them
  • Want to save a view (filter set)

Other

  • We had an opportunity to purchase cleaner data from a company called Serif, which would help with data cleanliness
  • Way to favorite a practice - feedback from founder

V2 follow-ups launched - June 2026

The following changes were made based on customer feedback from the first 4 months that Benchmark was launched.

  • Additional filters
  • Added column data
  • Easy way to see which codes are priced higher than your rates
  • Easier way to find your rates within all the rates
  • On histogram, way to shuffle between selected filters from parent page without having to go back
  • Way for marker to not read as marker on graph but indicator that a rate is included within a bar
  • Users didn't like expanding tables with empty column headers, looked like data was missing, used accordion pattern with labels
  • Truncate payer names to 10 characters
  • Easier way to see associated data in table, click opens up menu with ability to View data in table or drill into histogram
  • Freeze column headers when scrolling horizontally

Outcome

Benchmark V2 launched February 2026, followed by fast-follow improvements in June 2026 based on the first four months of customer feedback.

The results so far:

  • 127 MAUs in March 2026 — the highest in product history, reached within weeks of launch
  • Churn decreased 3 points in the four months following launch, with follow-up improvements shipped in June 2026 expected to continue that trend
The most impactful decision in this project wasn't a design pattern — it was pushing back on the favoriting feature and working with engineering to automatically surface all related NPIs for a practice. What started as a complex rule-builder became a single automated action, removing the problem entirely rather than designing around it.

Expedia Group

Competitiveness Opportunities

Goal: To make the requirements clearer to users on what needs to be done to improve their quality score, and in turn, increase their property's visibility on the site.

Role
Product Designer
Company
Expedia Group
Timeline
Add timeline

Background

Expedia's Partner Central uses a Quality Score to determine the visibility of a partner's property across Expedia Group's websites. This score is made up of several categories, and a property must be in good standing across all of them to achieve strong visibility on Partner Central. The categories are:

  • Competitive rates and availability (fka channel comparison)
  • Guest experience
  • Content score

The Channel Comparison service falls under the competitive rates and availability category. Expedia requires partners to keep their rates and inventory consistent with other online travel booking sites, such as Booking, Airbnb, and Priceline. This applies only when pricing is higher or inventory is missing on those other sites.

Channel Comparison checks other online travel agency sites to see whether their rates and inventory match ours. When inconsistencies are found, we alert the partner in Partner Central and provide proof in the form of an HTML snapshot. We also lower the partner's Quality Score, which impacts their visibility.

Booking offers a similar feature.

Problem

Channel Comparison doesn't give partners enough information or instruction on how to resolve the issues we want them to act on, including rate and availability discrepancies. Getting more detail requires a manual, frustrating deep-dive, and partners often feel we're comparing apples to pears and penalizing them unfairly as a result. This leaves partners frustrated, confused, and unlikely to take action on the issues we've flagged.

Process

Customer feedback & stakeholder interviews → Design exploration → Wireframes → Usability study → Final Fidelity

Customer quotes

"In Channel Comparison, Expedia is comparing the before taxes rates with after taxes rates."
"There is no any rate discrepancies for about a week already and the score is not updated yet."
"Not one of your rate comparison rates are for the same room as you compare it to another website."

Stakeholders interviews

  • Product: 5
  • Market Managers: 2
  • CI Business: 1
  • Engineering: 7

Findings from user feedback & interviews

  • Partners perceive the information as incorrect because they don't understand why we're comparing two different room types (apples to apples)
  • Partners don't understand why their Quality Score is poor when no issues are listed
  • Comparisons don't account for tax-excluded vs. tax-excluded pricing
  • Other OTAs offer deeper discounts, which is frustrating for connected properties
  • Caching issues cause the site to not reflect fixes partners have already made
  • Partners don't know when to expect changes to be reflected in their Quality Score (lack of a closing loop)
  • Partners are unaware that issues need attention
  • Connected properties won't update rates directly in Rate & Availability, instead making changes through their connectivity provider, which makes it harder for them to take action
  • Hotel comparisons are sometimes incorrect, e.g., comparing to a different hotel with the same name
  • Site takes a very long time to load

Flows and level of detail

We started with the most common issues that arise for customers: missing inventory and missing rate plan. There are 40 issues in total that will be addressed.

I mocked up a few layout options with varying detail, not knowing how much a customer would want to be able to fix the issue. We decided to go with a version with more detail.

Design solution included

  • Email and notification when issues arise.
  • Make it very clear what the root cause of the issue is and how to fix it.
  • Include timestamps on any HTML snapshot data.
  • Clearly indicate when an issue has been fixed, and let partners check back to confirm it's been resolved.
  • Show details of the issues
  • Show as much detail as possible, including an easy way for partners to double-check our work, with links to supporting data where applicable.

Usability findings

  • Grouping issues by root causes was understood well (5/5)
  • Partners want detail (5/5)
  • Links to the live sites are seen as useful for troubleshooting issue (5/5)
  • Partners are unlikely to take action unless they are certain the issue is real (and not "apples to pears") (5/5)
  • Partners want to see the actual price difference not just percent when price differences are displayed (3/5)
  • Partners will research the issue on the live sites themselves before deciding action to take (5/5)
  • Usability of in-place side panel to make the changes was not able to be confirmed; partners were too focused on wanting to see the issue details before taking action (5/5) (and not 'apples to pears')
